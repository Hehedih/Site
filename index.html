🪨 What is inside Earth?

Earth is made of several layers. Scientists learn about these layers by studying earthquakes, rocks, and how seismic waves travel through the planet.

🏔️

Crust

The thin, solid outer layer. Continents and ocean floors are part of the crust.

🔥

Mantle

A very thick layer of hot rock. Over long periods, some mantle rock moves slowly, helping drive plate motion.

🧲

Outer core

A layer of liquid metal, mostly iron and nickel. Its movement helps create Earth's magnetic field.

🌡️

Inner core

A solid metal center, mainly iron and nickel, kept solid by enormous pressure.

🌊 Earth's five oceans

Oceans influence weather and climate, move heat around the planet, and provide habitats for marine life. The Pacific is the largest and deepest ocean basin.

Pacific Ocean

The largest ocean, stretching between Asia and Australia on one side and the Americas on the other.

Atlantic Ocean

Separates the Americas from Europe and Africa.

Indian Ocean

Lies mainly between Africa, Asia, Australia, and the Southern Ocean.

Southern Ocean

Surrounds Antarctica and includes the powerful Antarctic Circumpolar Current.

Arctic Ocean

The smallest and shallowest of the five oceans, located around the Arctic region.

Did you know? Sunlight cannot reach the deepest parts of the ocean. Deep-sea organisms have special adaptations for life in darkness and under great pressure.

☁️ Earth's atmosphere

The atmosphere is the blanket of gases surrounding Earth. It is mostly nitrogen and oxygen, with smaller amounts of other gases, including carbon dioxide and water vapor.

Troposphere

The lowest layer. Most clouds and everyday weather happen here.

Stratosphere

Contains the ozone layer, which absorbs much of the Sun's harmful ultraviolet radiation.

Mesosphere

Many meteors heat up and break apart in this layer.

Thermosphere

A very thin region where auroras occur and some spacecraft orbit.

Exosphere

The outermost region, gradually fading into space.

🌦️ Weather and climate

Weather describes short-term conditions, such as today's temperature, rain, wind, or clouds. Climate describes the usual patterns of weather in a place over many years.

Energy from the Sun drives much of Earth's weather. The atmosphere and oceans move heat and moisture around the planet. Mountains, forests, ice, and land surfaces also affect local conditions.

A rainy afternoon is weather. The typical pattern of hot, rainy summers in a region is part of its climate.

🦋 Life on Earth

Living things are found in many environments, from tropical rainforests to deserts, polar regions, and deep oceans. An environment where an organism lives is called a habitat.

🌳 Forests

Trees and other plants provide food and shelter for birds, insects, mammals, fungi, and many more organisms.

🏜️ Deserts

Deserts receive little precipitation. Many plants and animals have adaptations that help them conserve water.

🧊 Polar regions

These cold environments include sea ice, glaciers, and wildlife adapted to low temperatures.

🪸 Coral reefs

Coral reefs are ocean habitats built by tiny animals called coral polyps. They support a great variety of marine life.

♻️ How can people help care for Earth?

People, communities, scientists, and governments can all help protect the natural world. Useful actions include:

  • Use only the water you need and fix leaks when possible.
  • Reduce waste by reusing items and recycling materials accepted locally.
  • Save electricity when practical.
  • Keep parks, rivers, and beaches free of litter.
  • Respect wildlife and avoid disturbing natural habitats.
  • Learn about environmental issues and discuss solutions using reliable information.

📚 Earth science word guide

Orbit

The path one object follows around another object in space. Earth orbits the Sun.

Rotation

The spinning of an object around its axis. Earth's rotation causes day and night.

Gravity

The attraction between objects that have mass. Earth's gravity keeps us and the atmosphere near the planet.

Ecosystem

A community of living organisms interacting with each other and with their nonliving surroundings.

Tectonic plate

A large piece of Earth's rigid outer shell that moves slowly over the mantle.
